Q1. How has your work at Cybage, Pune prepared you for architecting microservices in fintech?

Ans: During my tenure at Cybage, Pune, I have been extensively involved in both frontend and backend development for product-based modules using Monorepo and Micro-Frontend (MFE) architectures. This experience has sharpened my ability to design modular, scalable, and reusable components, which is essential for building microservices in the fintech sector.
At Cybage, I primarily worked on backend services using Nest.js, which is a progressive Node.js framework well-suited for building efficient, reliable, and scalable server-side applications. Nest.js incorporates modern design patterns like dependency injection and modularization that are fundamental for microservices architecture focusing on loose coupling and high cohesion.
Additionally, my work involved Jest testing, which improved my understanding of automated testing and continuous integration, critical for ensuring the reliability and quality of distributed microservices in production environments, especially in financial applications.
My exposure to Monorepo enabled me to manage and publish shared libraries efficiently, reducing code duplication and simplifying dependency management across microservices. This experience aligns well with managing enterprise-grade architectures with multiple interacting services.
Overall, working in an Agile environment, I collaborated with cross-functional teams to deliver high-quality solutions incrementally, which aligns with modern fintech development practices emphasizing rapid iteration to meet evolving customer needs.
