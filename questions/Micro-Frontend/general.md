Q1. What is your approach to implementing Micro-Frontend (MFE) architectures in fintech applications?

Ans: My experience with Micro-Frontend (MFE) architectures, especially at Cybage, Pune, centers on decomposing complex user interfaces into smaller, independent, and easily manageable frontend applications or modules.
MFE allows distributed teams to develop, test, and deploy frontend features autonomously while ensuring seamless integration into a cohesive user experience, crucial for fintech platforms supporting diverse business processes.
Implementing MFE enhances scalability by enabling parallel development, reduces overall technical debt, and isolates risks, which is essential when handling sensitive payment and financial data.
I emphasize using web components or module federation with frameworks like Angular and React.js, enabling reuse and independent lifecycle management of frontend parts.
The architecture aligns with the security protocols demanded in fintech by restricting attack surfaces and isolating faults without impacting the entire frontend system.
My approach ensures that individual Micro-Frontends adhere to consistent UI/UX guidelines and communication protocols, maintaining a unified customer interface critical for Mastercard’s global fintech solutions.
